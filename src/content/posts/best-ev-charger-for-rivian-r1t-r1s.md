Rivian owners have a simpler home-charging decision than almost anyone: every current R1T and R1S has an 11.5 kW onboard AC charger, and nothing you bolt to the wall can push past it. Buy any quality 48-amp charger with the right connector, feed it a 60-amp circuit, and you get the fastest home charging the truck can accept — about 25 miles of range per hour, and a full battery by morning.

## The one number that matters: 11.5 kW

Rivian's onboard charger — the AC-to-DC converter inside the truck — tops out at 11.5 kW. On 240 V single-phase, that's 48 amps. This is the ceiling no matter how expensive the wallbox is: an 80-amp charger won't charge a Rivian any faster than a 48-amp one, because the truck simply won't take more.

The speeds at each tier, per Rivian's own charging data:

| Charger | Power | Range added | Full charge (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 A hardwired | 11.5 kW | ~25 mi/hr | ~12 hours |
| 40 A plug-in | 9.6 kW | ~20 mi/hr | ~14 hours |
| 32 A mobile charger | 7.7 kW | ~15 mi/hr | ~18 hours |

The Rivian mobile charger that comes with the truck has a NEMA 14-50 adapter delivering 32 amps — fine as a backup, but a real home charger roughly doubles the speed. Given the R1T/R1S's big battery, most owners feel the difference and upgrade.

## What to look for in a Rivian charger

### 48 amps (and the circuit to feed it)

The whole point of this guide is the full 48 amps, and that means **hardwired on a dedicated 60-amp circuit** — the [80% continuous-load rule](/posts/40-amp-vs-48-amp-ev-charger/) again. A 48-amp plug-in charger doesn't exist in any meaningful sense: the NEMA 14-50 caps out at 40. If your panel can't support 60 amps, a 40-amp plug-in charger is the honest fallback — ~20 miles per hour still covers most driving — and our [panel capacity](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) and [breaker guides](/posts/what-size-breaker-for-ev-charger/) will tell you where you stand.

### Connector: J1772 is the native fit

R1T and R1S charge at home over the J1772 standard, so a J1772 charger plugs straight in with no adapters. Several chargers also ship in native-NACS variants — handy if you run a mixed household with a NACS-native vehicle, or want to future-proof. Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) walks through the trade-offs.

### Weather sealing and cable length

Rivians spend more time outdoors than the average EV — driveways, trailheads, unheated garages. Prioritize IP65/IP66 weather sealing, and buy the long cable: 24–25 ft. The charge port sits on the driver's side, and a short cable that works in a tidy garage fails the moment you park nose-in at a friend's place.

### Charge-port placement and cable routing

Rivians put the charge port on the driver's side, and with a truck this long, cable reach deserves real thought. A 25 ft cable handles a nose-in garage park or a post-mounted charger beside the driveway; a short cable doesn't, and extension cords are not an option for EV charging — ever. If the charger mounts on the far wall from the port, walk the route with a tape measure before you buy. Outdoor-rated cable jackets (standard on the IP66 units here) also survive being left on a driveway through weather.

### Time-of-use rates and scheduling

A Rivian's big battery is exactly the use case time-of-use (TOU) electricity plans reward: the difference between peak and off-peak rates can be 2–3x, and a full 11.5 kW charge pulls serious kilowatt-hours. Every charger in this guide supports scheduled charging in its app, so you can start the session at 11 PM automatically and let the truck fill by morning. Rivian's own app also supports charge scheduling, so you get two layers of control. If your utility offers an EV-specific rate plan, enroll — on a battery this size, the payback on a smart charger can land in months.

## The best chargers for a Rivian

### EVIQO Gen 2 — best value

The [EVIQO Gen 2](amazon:eviqo-gen2) (~$379–$429 street) is the value play that keeps winning this roundup: full 48 amps hardwired, IP66 weather sealing — the highest rating among the chargers we track — a 25 ft cable, and Wi-Fi scheduling in the app. For a Rivian that lives outdoors, the sealing and cable length check the two boxes that matter most, at about half the price of Rivian's own Wall Charger. Strong owner ratings back it up.

### Tesla Universal Wall Connector — best for mixed fleets

The [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](amazon:tesla-universal-wall-connector) (~$550–$600) delivers the full 48 amps with both NACS and J1772 connectors built in — flip the Magic Dock handle to switch. If your household has a Rivian and a Tesla (an extremely common pairing), this is the one box that charges both natively, no adapters. Slim 24 ft cable, Wi-Fi scheduling, power-sharing across up to six units if you ever add a second charger.

### ChargePoint Home Flex — most flexible

The [ChargePoint Home Flex](amazon:chargepoint-home-flex) (~$500–$600 street) goes to 50 amps hardwired — slightly above the Rivian ceiling, which costs you nothing since the truck self-limits — and adjusts 16–50 A in the app. The app is the draw: charge scheduling, energy tracking, and one login for ChargePoint's public network on road trips. Also sold as a 40-amp plug-in if you want portability instead of max speed.

### Grizzl-E Ultimate 48A — toughest 48-amp build

The [Grizzl-E Ultimate 48A](amazon:grizzl-e-ultimate-48a) (~$480–$650) is the heavy-duty 48-amp option: 25 ft cable, metal build, Wi-Fi with scheduling and load balancing across multiple units, and J1772 or native-NACS variants. If you want the simplest, most indestructible path to 48 amps — and load balancing for a future second EV — this is it.

### Emporia Pro — best with load management

If your panel is marginal, the [Emporia Pro](amazon:emporia-pro) (~$599) delivers 48 amps hardwired with Emporia's energy-monitoring ecosystem and load-management features — it can dynamically throttle charging to stay under your panel's limit. That's the difference between a panel upgrade and not one for some owners. Ships in J1772 or NACS.

## Rivian's own Wall Charger — do you need it?

Rivian's 48-amp Wall Charger ($800) is a perfectly good charger that will charge your Rivian at the full 11.5 kW. It just isn't meaningfully better than the third-party options above — the car negotiates the same 48 amps from any of them. You pay a premium for the Rivian branding and tighter app integration with the truck. If that integration matters to you, it's a fine purchase; if you want the same electrons for ~$400–$600, any J1772 48-amp unit on this list does it.

## Installation notes for Rivian owners

- **Permit and electrician:** a new 60-amp circuit and hardwired charger means a permit ($50–$200 typical) and a licensed electrician. This isn't the job to DIY.
- **Run the circuit once, right:** if there's any chance of a second EV, have the electrician size the run for future load balancing — our [installation cost and rebate guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) covers what's claimable.
- **Skip the 80-amp units:** they cost more, need bigger wire and breakers, and charge a Rivian exactly as fast as a 48-amp unit. The truck is the bottleneck.
- **Portable backup stays useful:** keep the included 32-amp mobile charger (or any portable Level 2 unit) in the frunk for destination charging — see our [portable charger guide](/posts/best-portable-level-2-ev-charger-renters/).

## Our take

Buy the full 48 amps — the Rivian's battery is big enough that you'll notice, and the install cost difference versus 40 amps is usually small. The [EVIQO Gen 2](/chargers/eviqo-gen2/) is the value pick, the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/) if there's a Tesla in the garage too, and the [ChargePoint Home Flex](/chargers/chargepoint-home-flex/) if you want the best app. Any of them fills the truck overnight; pick on price, weather sealing, and connector flexibility.
