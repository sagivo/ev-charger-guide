Two EVs in the driveway is increasingly normal — and it breaks the simple "buy one charger" logic. The up-front answer: **for most two-EV households, the Grizzl-E Duo (~$799–$999) is the cheapest clean solution** — one box, two cables, one 50-amp circuit, automatic power splitting. If both cars need full 48-amp speed or you already have panel headroom, **two power-sharing chargers** (Tesla or Wallbox) are the more capable answer.

There are three viable strategies. Which wins depends on your panel, your driving, and your budget.

## Strategy 1: One dual charger (Grizzl-E Duo)

The [Grizzl-E Duo](amazon:grizzl-e-duo) is the only mainstream home charger built for this exact job: two 24-foot J1772 cables, one NEMA 4 cast-aluminum box, and **intelligent power-sharing that needs no Wi-Fi or app** — it's automatic and hardware-based.

| | Spec |
|---|---|
| Total power pool | 40 A / ~10 kW (shared) |
| One car alone | Full 40 A |
| Two cars charging | ~20 A each, split automatically |
| Install | NEMA 14-50 plug — no hardwiring |
| Price | ~$799–$999 |
| Smart features | None (power-sharing is automatic) |

**The math that sells it:** two separate chargers on two circuits can easily run $1,000+ in hardware plus $500–$1,500 in electrician work for the second circuit — and often a panel upgrade on top. The Duo needs one 50-amp circuit, which many garages already have. Roughly half the total cost of the two-charger route.

**The trade-off:** 40 amps total is the ceiling. If both cars arrive empty on the same night, each gets ~20 amps (~15 mph) — still fine overnight, but not fast. And it's J1772-only, so Tesla owners use their included adapter. No app means no scheduling or energy tracking (your cars' own scheduling covers the off-peak part).

**Best for:** two-EV households on a single 50-amp circuit who want the cheapest path to charging both cars overnight.

## Strategy 2: Two chargers with power-sharing

If you want each car to charge at full speed whenever the other isn't plugged in, two networked chargers on power-sharing beat the Duo. They communicate to stay within a shared circuit limit, then give one car everything when it's alone.

- **Two Tesla Universal Wall Connectors** ([buy](amazon:tesla-universal-wall-connector), ~$550–$600 each): power-share across up to 6 units, 48 amps each when the other car is done, best-in-class app, 4-year warranty. Ideal if either car is a Tesla — the Magic Dock handles the other.
- **Two Wallbox Pulsar Plus 48A** ([buy](amazon:wallbox-pulsar-plus), ~$650–$800 each): power-sharing across up to **10 units** plus Power Boost load management, tiny footprint, voice control. The most scalable option — overkill for two cars, but the best multi-unit tech.
- **ChargePoint Home Flex pair** ([buy](amazon:chargepoint-home-flex)): load management support and the public-network app. 50 amps each is the fastest per-car ceiling here.

The catch is panel capacity: two 48-amp chargers on power-sharing still need a circuit sized for the shared limit plus your house load. Run the [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) before committing — and read [installation costs and rebates](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) for what the electrical work runs.

**Best for:** households where both cars regularly need fast turnarounds, or where the panel already supports the load.

## Strategy 3: Two independent circuits (no sharing)

Two chargers, two dedicated circuits, no communication between them. This is the simplest electrically — each charger gets its own breaker — but it's the most expensive: double the wire, double the breaker slots, and the highest panel load. It only makes sense if you already have two spare 50/60-amp circuits (rare) or you're doing a panel upgrade anyway.

For most homes this is overkill. Overnight charging gives you 8–10 hours; two cars almost never both need maximum speed simultaneously.

## The decision framework

| Your situation | Best strategy |
|---|---|
| One 50 A circuit, panel is tight | **Grizzl-E Duo** — both cars, no panel work |
| Panel has headroom, both cars drive a lot | **Two power-sharing chargers** (Tesla or Wallbox) |
| Two Teslas specifically | **Two Tesla Wall Connectors** with power-share — cheapest per unit |
| Older home, panel can't grow | **Duo**, or **Emporia Pro** with PowerSmart load management for a second charger |
| Might move in a few years | **Duo** (plug-in, comes with you) |

## The cost comparison, honestly

Let's put real numbers on the three strategies for a typical garage that already has one 50-amp circuit:

**Strategy 1 — Grizzl-E Duo:** ~$799–$999 for the unit + $0–$300 if the 14-50 outlet already exists (or $300–$800 for a fresh outlet install). **Total: ~$800–$1,800**, one circuit, no panel work.

**Strategy 2 — two power-sharing chargers:** two units at ~$450–$800 each = $900–$1,600, plus electrician work to wire the second unit and configure power-sharing: $500–$1,200. **Total: ~$1,400–$2,800** — before any panel upgrade, which can add $2,000–$4,000 if the panel can't take it.

**Strategy 3 — two independent circuits:** same hardware as Strategy 2 plus a second full circuit run ($800–$1,500) and the highest odds of triggering a panel upgrade. **Total: ~$2,200–$4,000+**, realistically more.

The Duo wins on cost in almost every scenario where the panel is the constraint. Strategy 2 only pulls ahead when you already have the electrical capacity — then the per-car speed and app features justify the premium.

## Don't forget the cars themselves

One underappreciated point: **your EVs' own scheduling covers most of what a smart dual-charger setup would do.** Every modern EV can schedule charging to off-peak hours from its own screen or app. So the Duo's lack of an app is a smaller loss than it looks — set the off-peak window in each car, plug both in, and the Duo's automatic splitting handles the rest.

What the cars can't do is coordinate with each other on a shared circuit — that's the Duo's hardware power-sharing or the networked chargers' software power-sharing. Either way, you're covered.

Also check connector compatibility before buying: the Duo is J1772-only. If one car is a Tesla, it charges through the J1772 adapter that comes with the car — slightly less convenient than a native NACS handle, but it works fine. If that friction bothers you daily, two [Tesla Universal Wall Connectors](amazon:tesla-universal-wall-connector) with their Magic Dock handles are the smoother (pricier) answer.

## One more option: stagger, don't share

Before buying any dual-charger hardware, sanity-check the simplest solution: **two plug-in chargers on one outlet, used at different times.** If one car charges overnight and the other charges during the day (or on alternating nights), a single 40-amp plug-in charger — or two cheap plug-ins sharing one NEMA 14-50 outlet on different schedules — covers both cars for the price of one circuit. It requires mild household coordination, but for couples with opposite schedules it's free. Only buy the Duo or a power-sharing pair once you've confirmed both cars genuinely need to charge simultaneously on a regular basis.

## Verdict

Start from the panel, not the charger. If a second circuit means a panel upgrade, the **Grizzl-E Duo** ends the conversation — it's purpose-built, plug-in, and roughly half the cost of the alternative. If your panel has room, **two Tesla Universal Wall Connectors on power-share** give you the best combination of per-car speed, app quality, and connector flexibility.
