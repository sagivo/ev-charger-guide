The charger is the cheap part. Here's what the whole project costs and how to cut it.

## Typical cost breakdown

| Item | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Charger unit | ~$350–$600 |
| Simple install (panel-adjacent) | $300–$800 |
| Long wire run / subpanel | $800–$1,500 |
| Panel upgrade (100 A → 200 A) | $1,500–$3,000 |
| Permit + inspection | $50–$200 |

The panel is the budget-killer. Before getting quotes, run our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) — if your panel can't spare the circuit, the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/) with load management may avoid the upgrade entirely.

## The 30% federal tax credit

The Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property credit covers 30% of the charger **and** installation costs, up to $1,000 for residential installs. Keep your electrician's itemized invoice — you'll need it at tax time. (Confirm current rules with a tax professional.)

## Utility rebates: check before you buy

Many utilities offer $300–$500+ rebates, discounted Time-of-Use rates, or both. Two gotchas: some rebates require specific charger models (often networked/smart chargers like the [ChargePoint Home Flex](/chargers/chargepoint-home-flex/)), and some require enrollment in a managed-charging program. Check your utility's EV page **before** purchasing — buying the wrong model can disqualify you.

## How to keep the install cheap

1. **Mount near the panel.** Every foot of 6-gauge copper wire costs money. The closest wall to the panel is the cheapest install.
2. **Consider plug-in.** A [NEMA 14-50 plug-in charger](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) on an existing or new outlet avoids hardwire labor.
3. **Right-size the amperage.** A 40-amp circuit is cheaper to add than a 60-amp one, and the charging difference is small overnight.
4. **Get 2–3 quotes.** Electrician pricing for EV circuits varies wildly — quotes for the same job can differ by 2x.
5. **Bundle with other electrical work.** If the electrician is already opening the panel, add the EV circuit then.

## Don't cheap out on the outlet

If you go plug-in, the NEMA 14-50 receptacle must be **industrial grade** (Hubbell or Bryant). Cheap dryer outlets have caused overheats and fires under continuous 40-amp EV loads. It's a ~$60–$100 part — non-negotiable.
