## What changed

For a decade, North America had two AC plugs: Tesla's proprietary connector and J1772 for everyone else. Starting with 2025 model-year vehicles, nearly every automaker switched new EVs to Tesla's design — now standardized as SAE J3400 and universally called NACS. Your next EV will almost certainly have a NACS port, and the public fast-charging network is converging on it too.

For home charging, this is simpler than it sounds. A Level 2 charger is just a smart 240 V outlet with a plug on the end — the car decides the rest. The only question is the plug's shape.

## The two plugs, side by side

**NACS** is compact — roughly the size of an Apple MagSafe puck scaled up — and carries both AC and DC through one connector. Every Tesla ever sold uses it, and since 2025, so does nearly every new EV from Ford, GM, Hyundai/Kia, Rivian, and the rest.

**J1772** is the larger, round five-pin plug that served every non-Tesla EV before the transition. Millions of J1772 cars are on the road and will be for another decade-plus, which is why J1772 home chargers are still manufactured and sold in volume.

Neither plug is "better" at home charging — at 240 V AC, electrons don't care about the shape. The decision is purely about matching your cars.

## What it means for your charger purchase

A home charger is a 10-year appliance, so buy for the cars you'll own across that span, not just today's:

- **All Teslas / 2025+ EVs:** buy NACS (or dual-connector). Native, no adapters. The [Tesla Wall Connector](amazon:tesla-wall-connector-gen3) (~$440–$475) is the benchmark here.
- **Older non-Tesla EV, keeping it for years:** J1772 is fine and often cheaper — the [Grizzl-E Classic](/chargers/grizzl-e-classic/) is J1772-only and excellent, and J1772 variants of most chargers run a little less than their NACS siblings.
- **Mixed household or undecided next car:** the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](amazon:tesla-universal-wall-connector) with its integrated Magic Dock nests a J1772 adapter inside the NACS handle — no separate adapter to buy, lose, or forget. It's the only charger in our [2026 ranking](/posts/best-level-2-ev-chargers-2026/) I'd call genuinely transition-proof.
- **J1772 charger today, NACS car tomorrow:** a ~$100–$120 NACS-to-J1772 adapter bridges the gap indefinitely. Plenty of households will run this setup for years.

## The transition timeline: what to expect next

The shift is essentially complete on the vehicle side — 2025 and 2026 model-year EVs from virtually every brand ship with NACS ports, and SAE's J3400 standardization means it's an open standard now, not Tesla's proprietary plug. Public charging is following: new DC fast-charging installations are predominantly NACS, and Tesla's Supercharger network has opened to other brands via adapters and native ports.

For your garage, the practical upshot is that a NACS or dual-connector charger bought today will match every new EV for the charger's entire ~10-year life. J1772 chargers aren't being obsoleted — they'll charge J1772 cars perfectly for as long as those cars run, and adapters cover everything else. The transition is a reason to choose deliberately, not a reason to wait.

## Adapters: the cheap bridge

Adapters work fine for AC home charging — this isn't DC fast charging, where adapter quality and thermal design matter far more. The two you might need:

- **J1772-to-NACS (~$50–$70):** lets any Tesla or NACS-native EV use a J1772 charger. Teslas ship with one in the car.
- **NACS-to-J1772 (~$100–$120):** lets an older J1772 EV use a NACS charger.

Keep the adapter with the car, not the charger — it lives in the frunk or trunk, ready for public J1772 stations too. Buy from a reputable brand (the charger makers and established EV accessory companies, not the cheapest no-name listing), and check that it's rated for your charger's amperage.

## Don't confuse the port with the speed

One misconception worth clearing up: NACS vs J1772 says nothing about how fast you'll charge at home. Both are AC connectors; your home charging speed is set by the charger's amperage, the circuit, and your EV's onboard charger — not the plug shape. A J1772 charger plus a $100 adapter charges a NACS car at exactly the same speed as a native NACS charger. The connector decision is about convenience and future-proofing, not performance.

## What about CHAdeMO?

If you drive a Nissan Leaf — the last CHAdeMO holdout in the US — note that CHAdeMO is a DC fast-charging plug, not a home-charging plug. Your Leaf charges at home on J1772 (Level 2) like every other older non-Tesla EV. The NACS transition doesn't change anything about your garage setup; it only affects which public fast chargers you'll use on road trips.

## How this affects resale and the long term

A NACS or dual-connector charger is the safer bet for home resale value in 2026 and beyond — the next buyer's EV will almost certainly be NACS. That said, a J1772 charger with a $100 adapter charges a NACS car exactly as fast as a native NACS charger does; the electrons are identical. Don't replace a working J1772 setup out of transition anxiety. Do buy NACS or dual when you're buying new.

**Bottom line:** connector choice is a 10-minute decision, not a reason to delay. Pick for your cars, keep an adapter in the frunk, and move on to the decisions that actually cost money — amperage and install.
