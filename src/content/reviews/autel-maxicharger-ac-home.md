The [Autel MaxiCharger AC Home](amazon:autel-maxicharger-ac-home) is the most feature-dense home charger you can buy: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, *and* Ethernet, a genuinely good app with off-peak optimization, and 50-amp / 12 kW output. One-line verdict: Autel has built automotive diagnostics since 2004 and it shows in the hardware — if you want the deepest connectivity and smart-charging stack in a home charger, this is it; if you want the best value, the AC Lite line or an EVIQO gets you 90% of it for less.

## Key specs in context

**50 amps / 12 kW hardwired — the fastest in this roundup on paper.** Roughly 40+ miles of range per hour for an efficient EV. The honest footnote, repeated because it matters: 50 vs 48 amps is ~4%, invisible overnight. What actually distinguishes the Autel is everything around the power delivery. That said, the 50-amp hardwired model at ~$599 sits in the same band as the ChargePoint Home Flex — price it against that, not against budget 48-amp units. And note the line structure: the 40-amp plug-in version covers simpler installs, while the **AC Lite** budget line (~$399 for 40 A plug-in, ~$569 for 50 A hardwired) carries the same smart stack with a thinner cable. Check the current price on the exact variant you want. Sanity-check the speed for your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**Triple connectivity: Wi-Fi + Bluetooth + Ethernet.** No other home charger here has Ethernet, and it's the sleeper feature of the whole roundup. Garages are where Wi-Fi goes to die — metal doors, distance from the router, concrete walls — and a charger that can't phone home can't update firmware or report energy use. Ethernet eliminates the most common smart-charger complaint (connectivity flakiness) at the root. Bluetooth covers local commissioning when the network is down.

**25-foot cable, J1772 standard with NACS on the 50 A hardwired.** Good reach; choose the connector to match your cars. Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) covers the decision.

**NEMA 4 outdoor, 3-year warranty.** Properly weather-sealed for exterior installs, with established-company backing behind the warranty. Autel's scale as an automotive supplier means parts and support are unlikely to vanish — a quiet reassurance smaller brands can't always offer.

## Installation

**Hardwired or NEMA 14-50 plug, depending on variant.** The 50-amp model is hardwired only (70-amp breaker for the full output — EV charging is a continuous load limited to 80% of the breaker rating); the 40-amp version comes as a plug-in. Amperage adjusts all the way down to 6 amps, the widest range here, which makes it unusually adaptable to constrained panels — dial it to whatever your panel can spare and verify with our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/). Price the electrician work with our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) and compare approaches in our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/). One install note: if you're running Ethernet to the garage, have the electrician pull it in the same trench/conduit run as the power — it's nearly free when the walls are already open.

## Smart features & app

The Autel Charge app is the real deal: scheduling, off-peak/time-of-use optimization that learns your rate structure, energy tracking, over-the-air firmware updates, optional RFID start/stop (handy if the charger is accessible to others), and dynamic load balancing. The TOU optimization is the standout — tell it your utility's rate plan and it minimizes cost automatically, which is real money every month on time-of-use rates. The honest gap: no OCPP or utility demand-response program support was found on the Home line, so utilities that pay you for managed charging may prefer a ChargePoint. Autel's diagnostics pedigree shows in thoughtful touches like detailed fault reporting — when something's wrong, the app tells you what, not just that.

## How it compares

Versus the [ChargePoint Home Flex](/chargers/chargepoint-home-flex/): the same 50-amp class, but the Autel's connectivity (Ethernet!) and app depth win for techies while ChargePoint's 274,000-station public network wins for road-trippers. If your garage has weak Wi-Fi, the Autel isn't close — it's the answer.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): the Emporia's whole-home load management (charger + panel monitor) is unique and can avoid a panel upgrade; the Autel's feature list is longer and its connectivity denser. Constrained panel: Emporia. Tech-forward with a good panel: Autel.

Versus the [Grizzl-E Ultimate 48A](/chargers/grizzl-e-ultimate-48a/): the Grizzl-E is tougher (cast aluminum, IP67) and cheaper, but its app is thinner and it lacks Ethernet. Rugged outdoor install: Grizzl-E. Connected smart home: Autel.

## Bottom line

No home charger packs more tech than the MaxiCharger AC Home — triple connectivity, the best TOU optimization in the class, 50-amp output, and an automotive company's build quality. At ~$399–$599 depending on variant (check the current price for the exact model you want), it's priced like the premium it is, and the 50-amp version needs a hardwired install. Budget buyers should look hard at the AC Lite line instead of the full Home — same smart stack, less money. If you want the most capable smart charger and your panel can feed it, this is the pick.
