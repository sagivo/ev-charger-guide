The [ChargePoint Home Flex](amazon:chargepoint-home-flex) is the most polished smart charger you can buy: the fastest output in its class at 50 amps / 12 kW, your choice of plug-in or hardwired install, and an app that also runs your public charging across 274,000+ ChargePoint stations. One-line verdict: if you road-trip on the ChargePoint network, the unified home-plus-road app makes this the premium pick worth paying for; if your car never leaves the garage, a Grizzl-E or EVIQO delivers nearly the same charging for meaningfully less.

## Key specs in context

**50 amps / 12 kW — the fastest mainstream home charger here.** In real terms that's roughly 40+ miles of range per hour for an efficient EV, or an empty-to-full overnight charge for even the largest battery packs. Honestly, though, the 50-versus-48-amp difference is about 4% — one or two miles of range per hour, invisible overnight. The practical reason the ceiling matters is off-peak windows: if your utility gives you only four or five cheap hours a night, the Home Flex squeezes the most miles into them. Use our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/) to see what 12 kW means for your specific EV.

**Choose your connector at purchase — and check the listing.** The Home Flex ships J1772 by default, and ChargePoint also sells a native-NACS version in both hardwired and plug-in form. They are separate Amazon listings, so confirm the connector before checkout; a wrong-connector return is an entirely avoidable headache. If you're unsure which you need, our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) is the five-minute read that prevents the most common regret in this category.

**23-foot cold-resistant cable.** A foot shorter than the class standard, but owners in cold climates specifically praise the jacket for staying flexible in winter — a real usability detail the spec sheet undersells, and one that matters every January morning.

**3-year warranty, UL listed indoor/outdoor.** Premium-tier coverage with no asterisks on the install location.

## Installation

The Home Flex is the flexible one: **hardwired or NEMA 14-50 plug**, your choice at purchase. The plug-in version is the move if you already have (or can cheaply add) a 14-50 receptacle — no hardwire labor, and the charger can unplug and move with you. Note the ceiling differs: hardwired unlocks the full 50 amps (on a 70-amp breaker, since EV charging counts as a continuous load limited to 80% of the breaker rating), while the plug-in version tops out at 40 amps on its 50-amp circuit — still plenty for overnight charging. Our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) walks through the trade-offs, and the [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) covers typical electrician pricing plus the federal tax credit. Amperage adjusts from 16 to 50 amps, so it can be dialed down for tighter panels — run our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) before assuming you need a panel upgrade.

## Smart features & app

This is where the Home Flex earns its premium. The ChargePoint app handles scheduling, charging reminders, energy and cost tracking, and Alexa integration — and because it's the same app as the public network, home and road charging live in one history and one bill. Off-peak scheduling is straightforward: tell it your utility's cheap hours and it handles the rest. The standout advantage versus rivals is **utility program compatibility** — the Home Flex is among the best-supported chargers for utility managed-charging programs and rebates, which can mean hundreds of dollars in incentives depending on your state. The honest caveat from owner reports: Wi-Fi setup can be fiddly, so do the initial pairing on a 2.4 GHz network with your phone in the garage and budget ten patient minutes.

## How it compares

Versus the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/): the Tesla wins on dual-connector convenience (Magic Dock — no adapter, ever) and raw app polish, but it's hardwired-only and lives outside the ChargePoint public network. If you charge publicly on ChargePoint stations, the Home Flex's unified app is the tiebreaker; if you don't, the Tesla is the better buy.

Versus the [Autel MaxiCharger AC Home](/chargers/autel-maxicharger-ac-home/): the Autel matches the 50-amp ceiling, adds Ethernet and Bluetooth, and typically costs less — the feature-maximalist's pick. The ChargePoint counters with the public-network app and stronger utility-program support. Techies lean Autel; road-trippers lean ChargePoint.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): the Emporia's PowerSmart load management can deliver 48 amps on a panel that couldn't otherwise support it — a bigger deal than any app feature if your panel is full. If your panel has room, the Home Flex's polish and public-network integration win. Our [Tesla vs ChargePoint vs Grizzl-E comparison](/posts/tesla-wall-connector-vs-chargepoint-home-flex-vs-grizzl-e/) puts the premium tier head to head.

## Bottom line

The Home Flex costs more than a Grizzl-E or Emporia and doesn't charge meaningfully faster than other 48–50-amp units — you pay for the best app experience, install choice, and the public-network integration. Street price runs ~$500–$600 (deal prices near $400 surface periodically), so check the current price rather than paying the top of the range. If you use ChargePoint's public stations, it's the obvious premium pick; if you don't, be honest with yourself about what the premium actually buys.
