The [EVIQO Gen 2](amazon:eviqo-gen2) is the sleeper pick of this roundup: a full 48-amp / 11.5 kW smart charger with the best weather sealing here (NEMA 4 / IP66), standout owner ratings, and street prices around $400. One-line verdict: it undercuts the big brands by $150+ while matching them on the specs that matter — the value play that doesn't feel like a compromise, as long as you can live with a merely okay app.

## Key specs in context

**48 amps / 11.5 kW for ~$400 street.** Let that sink in: the same charging ceiling as the ~$550 Tesla Universal and the ~$550 ChargePoint Home Flex. Roughly 35–44 miles of range per hour depending on the vehicle — empty-to-full overnight for any EV. At this power level the electrons don't care about the badge; what the premium brands sell is software polish and ecosystem, and the EVIQO's answer is "good enough hardware, great price." Verify it for your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**6–48 A adjustable in 1-amp increments.** The finest granularity in this roundup — via the app, plus a hardware DIP switch as backup. Why it matters: your electrician can set the charger to exactly what your panel can spare (say, 27 amps on an awkward circuit) instead of rounding down to a preset. That precision can be the difference between fitting on your existing panel and paying for an upgrade — check with our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/). Few chargers at any price offer this level of control.

**25-foot cable.** Full class-standard reach — no across-the-garage anxiety, unlike shorter-cable budget rivals.

**NEMA 4 / IP66 — the best weather sealing here.** Dust-tight and protected against powerful water jets. For an exposed outdoor or driveway install, this is a genuine edge over IP65-rated competitors, and it's backed by real certifications: UL, ETL, FCC, ENERGY STAR, plus a 3-year warranty with US-based support. Salt air, dust storms, pressure washers — the Gen 2 is rated for abuse its price doesn't suggest.

## Installation

**Hardwired (48 A) or NEMA 14-50 plug-in (40 A) — your choice.** The hardwired version delivers the full 11.5 kW on a 60-amp breaker; the plug-in gives 40 amps with no hardwire labor and the ability to move. They're separate listings, so buy the variant that matches your install plan. Our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) walks through the decision, and our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) covers electrician pricing plus the federal tax credit. The 1-amp granularity is the install superpower here — have your electrician check the panel math first.

## Smart features & app

The honest assessment first: MotorTrend's "great hardware, not so great app" verdict is fair. The Wi-Fi app covers the essentials — remote start/stop, scheduling, off-peak charging, energy and cost tracking, OTA firmware updates, charge reminders — and the enhanced Wi-Fi antenna helps in detached garages. It all works; it's just less polished than Tesla's or ChargePoint's software, with occasional UI quirks reported by owners. The counterweight is the hardware: reviewers call out genuinely nice touches you don't expect at $400 — a rubberized handle with a metal latch, a side reset button, a fluorescent holster for nighttime visibility, a steel-shielded mainboard. If the app is your top priority, the [ChargePoint Home Flex](/chargers/chargepoint-home-flex/) or [Autel MaxiCharger](/chargers/autel-maxicharger-ac-home/) are more polished — and cost $150+ more.

**What the owner ratings tell you.** 4.8 stars across 1,500+ Amazon ratings is an unusually strong signal for a smaller brand — that volume is hard to game, and it suggests the hardware reliability and US-based support are genuinely delivering. When a budget product holds ratings like that over time, it usually means the company is handling the unglamorous stuff (replacements, troubleshooting) well.

## How it compares

Versus the [Lectron V-BOX Pro](/chargers/lectron-vbox-pro/): similar price and the same 48 amps, but the EVIQO has a 25-foot cable (vs 16), better weather sealing (IP66 vs IP65), stronger owner ratings, and a 3-year warranty (vs 1). The Lectron is better-looking; the EVIQO is the safer default.

Versus the [Grizzl-E Classic](/chargers/grizzl-e-classic/): the Classic is tougher and ~$80–$130 cheaper, but it's 40 amps, J1772-only, and app-free. The EVIQO is the "I want the tank's specs plus an app" answer for barely more money.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): the Emporia's whole-home load management is unique and can avoid a panel upgrade — worth ~$200 more if your panel is the constraint. If your panel has room, the EVIQO delivers the same 48 amps for less.

## Bottom line

EVIQO's Gen 2 is the value play that doesn't feel like one: full 48-amp output, the best weather sealing in this roundup, thoughtful hardware details, strong owner ratings, and street prices under $430 — buy at the street price, never the $514 MSRP. The app is the weak spot, but at this price the hardware wins the argument. Check the current price and confirm the connector (J1772 or NACS) on the listing before buying.
