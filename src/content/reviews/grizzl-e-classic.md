The [Grizzl-E Classic](amazon:grizzl-e-classic) is the value/ruggedness champion: a UL-listed, made-in-Canada, cast-aluminum tank of a charger for ~$300–$450. It's deliberately dumb — no app, no Wi-Fi — and that's the point. One-line verdict: if you want a charger you install once and forget for a decade, and you'd rather spend money on metal than software, nothing else in this roundup touches it.

## Key specs in context

**40 amps / 9.6 kW — roughly 30 miles of range per hour.** Here's the honest truth about amperage: 40 amps charges any EV from empty to full overnight. The jump to 48 amps only matters inside narrow off-peak windows or for the very largest battery packs. Most buyers paying extra for 48 amps will never notice it — so the Classic's 40-amp ceiling is a real-world non-issue, not a compromise. Run your car through our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/) and see for yourself.

**DIP-switch amperage adjustment (16/24/32/40 A).** This is the Classic's quiet superpower: you set the current with physical switches inside the unit to match your circuit — no app, no account, no firmware. An electrician can commission it for a 30-amp dryer circuit or a full 50-amp run, and it stays code-compliant either way. At this price, adjustable amperage is rare.

**24-foot cable, NEMA 4 / IP67 cast aluminum.** The enclosure is the story: dust-tight, submersible-rated, corrosion-proof metal. Mount it outside in a blizzard and forget it. The honest caveat is the cable can be stiff in extreme cold — the price of a heavy-duty jacket.

**J1772 only — the one thing to think about.** There's no NACS variant of the Classic. Tesla owners use the adapter that came with the car (it works fine), but if your household is moving to native-NACS EVs, budget for adapters or consider the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/). Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) covers the trade-off.

**The longevity argument.** There's a case for the Classic that doesn't show up on spec sheets: fewer components, no cloud dependency, no firmware to brick, and a metal enclosure mean there's almost nothing to fail. Chargers are a 10–15-year purchase, and the Classic is the unit most likely to still be working — unchanged — a decade from now. If you've ever fought a "smart" appliance that got worse with updates, you know exactly why that matters.

## Installation

This is the easiest install in the roundup: **NEMA 14-50 plug**. No hardwiring, no electrician landing lugs — if you have a 14-50 receptacle on a 50-amp circuit, you hang the unit and plug it in. If you don't have the receptacle yet, adding one is the cheapest possible EV install; our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) has typical pricing plus the federal tax credit. One pro tip from the field: use an industrial-grade receptacle (Hubbell/Bryant), not a cheap dryer outlet — EV charging is a continuous multi-hour load, and bargain outlets are the most common failure point in plug-in installs. Compare approaches in our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/), and verify your panel's headroom with the [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/).

## Smart features & app

None — deliberately. The base Classic has no Wi-Fi, no app, no scheduling, no energy stats. If that sounds like a missing feature, this isn't your charger. If it sounds like a relief — no accounts, no firmware updates, no cloud dependency, nothing to break — you understand the product. Your car's own charge scheduling (every EV has it) covers off-peak timing just fine. And if you change your mind, the Wi-Fi "Classic Connect" version adds the app to the same hardware — though at that price, cross-shop the [EVIQO Gen 2](/chargers/eviqo-gen2/) first.

## How it compares

Versus the [EVIQO Gen 2](/chargers/eviqo-gen2/): for ~$80–$130 more, the EVIQO adds 48 amps, a real app, and even better weather sealing (IP66). If you want *any* smarts at all, the EVIQO is the better buy. If you want the toughest dumb box, the Classic wins.

Versus the [Lectron V-BOX Pro](/chargers/lectron-vbox-pro/): similar money, but the Lectron is a 48-amp smart charger with a short 16-foot cable and a lighter build. The Classic is the "buy it for life" pick; the Lectron is the "more specs per dollar" pick.

Versus the [Grizzl-E Ultimate 48A](/chargers/grizzl-e-ultimate-48a/): the Ultimate is the same toughness philosophy plus 48 amps, an app, and a NACS option for ~$200+ more. The Classic is for buyers who looked at the Ultimate's app and thought "I will never open that."

## Bottom line

The Grizzl-E Classic is the charger you buy when you want the toughest box for the least money and couldn't care less about an app. UL listed, built like a tank in Canada, DIP switches for any circuit, plug-in install, ~$300–$450 street. The trade-offs — J1772-only, no software, stiff winter cable — are all disclosed upfront and none of them matter if you want install-and-forget. Check the current price; it floats in the budget band and deals surface regularly.
