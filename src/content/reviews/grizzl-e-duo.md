The [Grizzl-E Duo](amazon:grizzl-e-duo) solves a specific expensive problem: two EVs, one 50-amp circuit, no panel upgrade. It plugs into a single NEMA 14-50 outlet and charges two cars at once, automatically balancing its 40-amp pool between them. One-line verdict: at ~$799–$999 it's roughly half the cost of two separate chargers plus the electrical work you'd otherwise need — but only if you actually have two EVs and a constrained panel.

## Key specs in context

**40 amps shared — up to ~10 kW to one car, split when two charge.** This is the spec to understand properly. Alone, one car gets the full 40 amps (roughly 30 miles of range per hour — a full overnight charge). With both cars plugged in, the Duo balances the pool between them automatically, so each gets roughly half. For the canonical use case — two commuters plugged in overnight — both cars gain 100+ miles by morning, which covers the vast majority of two-EV households. What the Duo can't do is fast daytime turnarounds: if one car needs a quick midday top-up while the other is charging, you're splitting. Be honest about your driving patterns before buying.

**Dual J1772 handles, two 24-foot cables.** Two full-length premium cables means both cars reach without swapping handles or playing parking Tetris — a daily-friction detail that matters more than it sounds. Both handles are J1772 with no NACS variant; Tesla owners use the adapter that came with the car. Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) covers the connector landscape. Note the Duo's handles are fixed at 24 feet each — plan your mount point so both cars' charge ports fall within reach.

**The math that sells it.** Two separate 40-amp chargers need two 50-amp circuits. If your panel can't take a second circuit, that's a panel upgrade at $1,500–$3,000 — suddenly a ~$900 Duo looks like a bargain. Run your panel through our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) and price the alternative with our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) before deciding; the Duo wins when the panel is the constraint.

**NEMA 4 / IP67 cast aluminum, 3-year warranty.** The same tank-like Grizzl-E build as the single-car units — this thing will outlive the cars.

**The overnight math, honestly.** Take two typical commuters each driving 40 miles a day: that's roughly 12 kWh per car to replace. The Duo's shared 40 amps delivers ~9.6 kW, so even split, both cars recover their daily usage in about 75 minutes. The sharing only pinches on the rare days both cars arrive near-empty — and even then, an overnight window is long enough for both to reach full. The Duo's ceiling is a problem for road-trip turnaround days, not for daily life.

## Installation

**NEMA 14-50 plug — one circuit, no hardwiring.** That's the entire value proposition in install terms: a single 50-amp circuit with a 14-50 or 6-50 receptacle, and you're done. No hardwire labor, no second homerun, no panel work. As with any plug-in install, use an industrial-grade receptacle (Hubbell/Bryant) — EV charging is a continuous load and cheap dryer outlets are the most common failure point. Our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) has the full picture.

## Smart features & app

None — and that's by design, not omission. The power-sharing is hardware-automatic: plug in one car, it gets everything; plug in two, they split. No Wi-Fi to configure, no app to update, no cloud account that can break. For off-peak scheduling, every EV has built-in charge timing — set it in the car, not the charger. If you need app-controlled scheduling or energy tracking per vehicle, the Duo isn't your product; two separate smart chargers are.

## How it compares

Versus two [Grizzl-E Classics](/chargers/grizzl-e-classic/): the Duo costs less than two Classics and needs one circuit instead of two — but two Classics give each car a dedicated 40 amps with no sharing. If your panel takes two circuits, two separate chargers charge faster.

Versus two [EVIQO Gen 2s](/chargers/eviqo-gen2/): similar story — two EVIQOs cost roughly the same as one Duo (~$400 × 2) and deliver 48 amps each with apps, *if* your panel supports two 60-amp circuits. Panel room is the whole decision.

Versus [Tesla Wall Connectors](/chargers/tesla-wall-connector-gen3/) with power-sharing: Tesla's setup is smarter (app control, 48 amps per car when alone) and shares up to six units on one circuit — but it's Tesla-only, hardwired, and costs more per car. Mixed-EV households on one circuit: Duo. Tesla-only: Wall Connectors.

## Bottom line

The Duo is a niche product that is perfect inside its niche: two EVs, one 50-amp circuit, no appetite for a panel upgrade. The 40-amp shared ceiling and zero smart features are real limitations — don't buy it for one EV, and don't buy it if your panel has room for two circuits. But if the alternative is a $2,500 panel upgrade, the ~$799–$999 Duo (check the current price; it fluctuates) is the cheapest way to charge two cars. Just verify your panel math first.
