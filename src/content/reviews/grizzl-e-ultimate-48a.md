The [Grizzl-E Ultimate 48A](amazon:grizzl-e-ultimate-48a) is the enthusiast's pick: Grizzl-E's famously tank-like cast-aluminum build with a real smart app, your choice of J1772 or native-NACS connector, and a full 48 amps — for ~$480–$650, undercutting Tesla and ChargePoint on price. One-line verdict: if you want the toughest 48-amp smart charger and don't need the Tesla app's polish or a plug-in install, this is the sweet spot of the whole roundup.

## Key specs in context

**48 amps / 11.5 kW — the full home-charging ceiling.** Roughly 35–44 miles of range per hour depending on the vehicle, which means any EV charges empty-to-full overnight with hours to spare. At this power level the differences between brands are single-digit percentages; what you're choosing is build quality, software, and connector — and that's where the Ultimate's case is strongest. Sanity-check your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**J1772 or native NACS — your choice at purchase.** Unlike the Tesla Universal's integrated Magic Dock, the Ultimate makes you pick one connector. That's a genuine limitation in a transitional market: choose wrong and you're buying an adapter later. Match the connector to the cars you'll own for the next five-plus years, and read our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) if the garage's future is uncertain.

**25-foot cable — the longest in the class.** One of those specs that matters more than it sounds: an extra foot or two is the difference between reaching the far bay of a two-car garage and not. The honest caveat from owner feedback is that the cable runs stiffer in cold weather than the Classic's — a minor annoyance, not a flaw. If your winters are brutal, plan the mount point to minimize how much cable you handle daily.

**NEMA 4X / IP67 cast aluminum, UL tested.** This is the toughest enclosure rating here — dust-tight, hose-down-proof, corrosion-resistant. If the charger lives on an exposed exterior wall through winters and storms, the Ultimate is built for exactly that.

**A word on the 80-amp sibling.** Grizzl-E also sells an 80 A / 19.2 kW Ultimate for ~$899, and it's worth knowing why you almost certainly don't want it: it needs a 100-amp circuit, and almost no EV on the road can accept 80 amps of AC charging anyway. The 48-amp version is the full practical ceiling — the 80-amp exists for commercial duty cycles and bragging rights, not homes.

## Installation

Hardwired only — the hardwire kit (terminal lugs, heat-shrink) is included in the box, but you still need a licensed electrician to land it on a dedicated circuit. Full 48 amps needs a 60-amp breaker; the amperage adjusts from 16 to 48 amps, so it can be commissioned lower for tighter panels — our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) will tell you where you stand before you call the electrician. Budget the install labor on top of the unit price with our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) (federal tax credit included), and weigh the approach against our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) — if you need plug-in, the Ultimate isn't your charger.

## Smart features & app

The Grizzl-E Connect app covers scheduling, energy statistics, remote control, and diagnostics, plus intelligent load distribution across multiple Ultimates (equal or priority sharing for two-EV garages). It works with or without an internet connection, and the external Wi-Fi antenna gives it better range than most — a thoughtful touch for detached garages. The honest assessment: the app is good and genuinely useful, but it's not the Tesla or ChargePoint app — less polished, a smaller ecosystem, no public-charging network attached. What you get instead is the metal box and the price.

## How it compares

Versus the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/): similar money, a tougher enclosure, and a native-NACS option — but no integrated dual connector and a thinner app ecosystem. If your garage will see both connector types, the Tesla's Magic Dock wins; if it's one connector and harsh weather, the Grizzl-E wins.

Versus the [Grizzl-E Classic](/chargers/grizzl-e-classic/): the Classic is the same toughness philosophy for ~$200 less, but it's 40 amps, J1772-only, and deliberately app-free. The Ultimate is the "I want the tank *and* the app" upgrade — worth it if you'll actually use scheduling and energy stats.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): the Emporia's whole-home load management is unique and can avoid a panel upgrade entirely; the Grizzl-E's build is tougher and it typically costs less. Full panel? Take the Grizzl-E. Constrained panel? Take the Emporia. Our [Tesla vs ChargePoint vs Grizzl-E comparison](/posts/tesla-wall-connector-vs-chargepoint-home-flex-vs-grizzl-e/) frames the premium tier.

## Bottom line

The Ultimate 48A is the charger for buyers who distrust plastic boxes and subscription-feeling apps: a metal, weatherproof, UL-tested unit with honest smart features and your choice of connector, for less than the big names. Hardwired-only install and a single-connector choice are the trade-offs. Check the current price — it floats in the ~$480–$650 band — and make sure you're looking at the 48A version, not the pricier 80A.
