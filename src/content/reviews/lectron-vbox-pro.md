The [Lectron V-BOX Pro](amazon:lectron-vbox-pro) is the design-led budget play: full 48-amp / 11.5 kW smart charging for ~$370–$400, in a genuinely good-looking package with a distinctive V-shaped LED status indicator. One-line verdict: Lectron made its name on EV adapters and brought that value ethos to home charging — it's a lot of charger for the money, as long as your parking spot is close to the install location.

## Key specs in context

**48 amps / 11.5 kW for ~$370–$400.** The same charging ceiling as units costing twice as much — roughly 35–44 miles of range per hour depending on the vehicle, empty-to-full overnight. The electrons truly don't care about the badge at this point; the V-BOX Pro is proof that 48-amp hardware has become a commodity. What varies is everything around the power: cable, software, warranty, build. Check your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**16-foot cable — the whole catch.** This is the shortest cable in the roundup and the spec that decides the purchase. Sixteen feet is plenty when the charger mounts on the wall right next to the charge port — and Lectron is betting most buyers' setups look like that. But it rules out across-the-garage installs, far-bay parking, and driveway charging from a garage-mounted unit. Measure your actual parking distance with a tape measure before buying; "probably fine" is how you end up with a return. Rivals give you 23–25 feet for similar money, so the Lectron only wins this comparison if the mount point is close. Pro tip: mock up the run with a 16-foot rope from the planned mount location to the car's charge port before you order — it takes two minutes and eliminates the guesswork.

**Separate J1772 and NACS variants — no adapter needed.** Pick the connector that matches your cars at purchase. Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) covers the call if you're torn.

**16–48 A adjustable, IP65 outdoor, UL/ETL + FCC + ENERGY STAR.** The certifications are real — this isn't a no-name fire hazard, it's a certified charger from an established EV accessories company that has been selling charging gear for years. The 1-year warranty, though, is the shortest here (rivals offer 3, Tesla 4) — you're accepting less manufacturer backing, priced accordingly.

## Installation

**Hardwired (48 A) or NEMA 14-50 plug-in (40 A).** Full power needs a 60-amp breaker and an electrician; the plug-in version gives 40 amps with no hardwire labor. Our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/) covers the trade-offs, and our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) has electrician pricing plus the federal tax credit. Amperage adjusts across 16/24/32/40/48-amp steps to match your circuit — verify headroom with our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/). The short cable actually simplifies install planning: mount it close to the port and the run is trivial.

## Smart features & app

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth with the Lectron app: remote scheduling, real-time charge progress, and kWh charge caps (set it to add exactly 20 kWh, for example — a nice touch for managing costs). It covers the essentials and stays out of your way. What's missing versus the premium apps: no utility program integration, no OCPP, no public-charging network, no energy-cost analytics worth mentioning. It's a competent basic app, not a smart-home platform — which is fine at this price, as long as that's what you expect.

**The kWh cap is the underrated feature.** Setting the charger to add exactly 20 kWh is more useful than it sounds: it lets you top up to a precise level without relying on the car's charge-limit settings, which is handy for households sharing one charger between cars with different needs, or for anyone on a utility plan where every kilowatt-hour above a threshold costs more.

## How it compares

Versus the [EVIQO Gen 2](/chargers/eviqo-gen2/): the EVIQO costs about the same with a 25-foot cable, better sealing (IP66), stronger owner ratings, and a 3-year warranty. The Lectron wins on looks; the EVIQO wins on nearly everything else. The EVIQO is the safer default unless the design genuinely matters to you.

Versus the [Grizzl-E Classic](/chargers/grizzl-e-classic/): similar money, opposite philosophies — the Lectron is a 48-amp smart charger in a light enclosure; the Classic is a 40-amp dumb charger in a cast-aluminum tank. Want specs and software: Lectron. Want install-and-forget durability: Classic.

Versus the [Tesla Wall Connector (Gen 3)](/chargers/tesla-wall-connector-gen3/): the Tesla costs ~$70+ more for the same 48 amps but brings the best app in the category, a 24-foot cable, and a 4-year warranty. Tesla households should weigh that seriously.

## Bottom line

Lectron's V-BOX Pro delivers the full 48-amp experience for little more than half the price of the big brands, in the best-looking budget package here. The trade-offs are real but manageable: a short 16-foot cable (measure first), a basic app, and a 1-year warranty. If your parking spot is close to the install location and you want maximum amps per dollar with some style, it's a lot of charger for ~$370–$400 — check the current price and confirm the connector variant before buying.
