The [Tesla Wall Connector](amazon:tesla-wall-connector-gen3) is the benchmark home charger for Tesla households: 48 amps, 11.5 kW, the best charging app in the business, and a 4-year warranty for around $440–$475. One-line verdict: if every car in the driveway is a Tesla and will stay that way, this is the charger to buy — nothing charges a Tesla better for the money, and the only real limitation is the NACS-only connector.

## Key specs in context

**48 amps / 11.5 kW — about 44 miles of range per hour to a Tesla.** That's the practical ceiling of home AC charging: a Model Y Long Range goes from 10% to 90% in roughly five to six hours, so even the cheapest overnight off-peak window covers a full charge. You don't buy 48 amps for speed you'll feel daily; you buy it so charging time is never the constraint, and so you can stack charging into whatever narrow cheap-rate window your utility offers. See what it means for your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**NACS only — the one real limitation.** The Gen 3 has a native Tesla connector and nothing else. Non-Tesla EVs need a separate NACS-to-J1772 adapter (not included), which works fine but is one more thing to buy, keep track of, and occasionally leave in the wrong car. In 2026 this matters less than it used to — most new EVs ship with NACS ports — but if a J1772-port car ever uses the charger, read the [Universal Wall Connector review](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/) before deciding. Our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/) covers the transition.

**24-foot cable, thin and light.** Owners consistently call out the cable as the best-handling in the category — notably thinner and lighter than rivals' 48-amp cables, which matters every time you coil it in winter with gloves on.

**4-year warranty, ENERGY STAR certified.** The longest standard warranty in this roundup, and the efficiency certification keeps standby draw honest.

**A note on the "Tesla tax" question.** At ~$440–$475 the Gen 3 is priced like a mid-tier charger, not a luxury one — it undercuts the ChargePoint Home Flex while matching or beating it on app quality and warranty length. The value proposition only breaks if you need a plug-in install (it doesn't offer one) or a non-NACS connector. For its intended buyer, it's the rare product that's both the best and among the cheapest.

## Installation

Hardwired only — there is no plug-in Gen 3. A licensed electrician wires it to a dedicated circuit: 60-amp breaker for the full 48 amps. Factor install labor into the budget using our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/), which also covers the federal tax credit that can offset it, and compare the approach with our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/). The amperage is adjustable from 12 to 48 amps at commissioning, so a smaller panel isn't a dealbreaker — 32 amps on a 40-amp circuit still delivers roughly 30 miles of range per hour. Check your actual headroom with our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/) before assuming the worst. The enclosure is rated for indoor and outdoor installation (NEMA 3R-type), so an exterior wall or driveway mount is fine.

## Smart features & app

The Tesla app is the category benchmark: charge scheduling tied to your utility's cheap hours, live energy monitoring, charge stats and history, remote start/stop, and firmware that arrives over the air — all on a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi connection. It is, frankly, built for Tesla owners first; non-Tesla app features are limited compared with ChargePoint or Emporia. The standout hardware feature is **power-sharing**: up to six Wall Connectors can coordinate on a single circuit, splitting available current automatically. That's the default setup for two-Tesla garages and a genuinely elegant answer to limited panel capacity — no app configuration, it just works.

## How it compares

Versus the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/): identical 48-amp speed, the same app, the same 4-year warranty, the same power-sharing — for ~$100+ less. The Universal's Magic Dock is the only difference. All-Tesla forever? Save the money. Any doubt? The Universal is the insurance policy.

Versus the [ChargePoint Home Flex](/chargers/chargepoint-home-flex/): the ChargePoint adds a plug-in option and a unified public-charging app, and peaks at 50 amps — but costs more, and for Tesla owners the Tesla app's polish and vehicle integration win. Road-trippers on the ChargePoint network may still prefer the Flex.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): the Emporia's whole-home load management can unlock 48 amps on a panel the Tesla would need dialed down on — a real advantage in older homes. But it's ~$150+ more and the Tesla app remains better. Our [Tesla vs ChargePoint vs Grizzl-E comparison](/posts/tesla-wall-connector-vs-chargepoint-home-flex-vs-grizzl-e/) goes deeper.

## Bottom line

The Gen 3 Wall Connector is the highest-rated EV charger on Amazon for a reason: full 48-amp speed, the best app, power-sharing, and a 4-year warranty at a mid-tier price of ~$440–$475. Its only weakness is the NACS-only connector in a transitional market — answer that question honestly before buying, and if the answer is "maybe," the Universal is worth the premium. Otherwise, this is the Tesla-household default, and check the current price before checkout.
