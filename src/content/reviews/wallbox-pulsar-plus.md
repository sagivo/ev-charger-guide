The [Wallbox Pulsar Plus](amazon:wallbox-pulsar-plus) is the compact-premium pick: a 48-amp / 11.5 kW charger the size of a sandwich, with multi-unit power sharing (up to 10 units) that nobody else matches. One-line verdict: at ~$650–$800 it's the priciest unit in this roundup — you're paying for the form factor and the smarts, not the electrons, so it only makes sense for design-conscious or multi-EV households.

## Key specs in context

**48 amps / 11.5 kW — same ceiling as chargers costing half as much.** Roughly 35–44 miles of range per hour depending on the vehicle; empty-to-full overnight. Let's be direct: a $400 EVIQO moves the same electrons. The Pulsar Plus premium buys everything *around* the power — size, software, sharing intelligence, voice control. If that sentence doesn't excite you, stop here and buy the cheaper charger. Verify the speed for your car with our [charge time calculator](/tools/charge-time-calculator/).

**7.8 × 7.9 × 3.9 inches, 4.4 lb — the smallest 48-amp charger here, by far.** This is the reason the Pulsar Plus exists. Most 48-amp chargers are chunky wall boxes; the Pulsar Plus is closer to a thick paperback. On a finished garage wall, a small condo parking spot, or anywhere aesthetics matter, it visually disappears where a Grizzl-E looks like industrial equipment. Assembled in the USA, UL listed — the small size doesn't mean cut corners.

**Power sharing across up to 10 units + Power Boost.** This is the most sophisticated multi-charger coordination in the roundup: up to ten Pulsar Plus units can share limited electrical capacity, splitting current automatically, while Power Boost does whole-home load management (dialing charging back when the house needs the power). For a three-EV household on a constrained panel, this is genuinely the best-engineered answer. For one car, it's capability you'll never touch.

**25-foot cable, J1772 standard with NACS option, 6–48 A adjustable.** Full reach, your choice of connector (see our [NACS vs J1772 explainer](/posts/nacs-vs-j1772-explained/)), and fine-grained amperage to fit tight panels — check yours with our [panel capacity checker](/tools/panel-capacity-checker/).

## Installation

**Hardwired only at 48 amps** — a licensed electrician lands it on a dedicated 60-amp breaker for full output. The 40-amp model offers a NEMA 14-50 plug-in if you need it. The small size is an install advantage in tight spots: it fits where bigger boxes won't, and the light weight makes one-person mounting easy. Price the electrician work with our [installation cost guide](/posts/level-2-ev-charger-installation-cost-rebates/) (federal tax credit included) and compare approaches in our [hardwired vs plug-in guide](/posts/hardwired-vs-plug-in-ev-charger/). NEMA 4 rated, so exterior installs are fine.

## Smart features & app

The myWallbox app covers scheduling, energy monitoring, notifications, session history, and remote lock (disable charging when you're away — useful for shared parking), plus Alexa/Google voice control and offline-capable charger intelligence. The honest history: early-2024 firmware and app issues earned the brand some rough reviews, and Wallbox has fixed them via over-the-air updates — 2025–2026 feedback skews notably more positive. But software remains the brand's historical weak spot versus Tesla or ChargePoint; the hardware is the safer part of the bet. If app polish is your top criterion, the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/) is cheaper and better on software.

**Where the Pulsar Plus quietly wins: shared and visible installs.** The remote-lock feature (disable the charger from the app) matters in condo garages and shared driveways where anyone could plug in on your dime. And the tiny footprint isn't just aesthetics — in tight parking spots with the charger mounted at bumper height, a 4.4-lb box is simply less of an obstacle than a 15-lb one. These are the details that justify the premium for apartment and condo dwellers, not just design enthusiasts.

## How it compares

Versus the [Autel MaxiCharger AC Home](/chargers/autel-maxicharger-ac-home/): the Autel gives you 50 amps and denser connectivity (Ethernet!) for less money, but can't match the Pulsar's size or 10-unit sharing. Tech-maximalist with one car: Autel. Design-conscious or multi-EV: Wallbox.

Versus the [Tesla Universal Wall Connector](/chargers/tesla-universal-wall-connector/): the Tesla is ~$100–$250 cheaper with a better app and the Magic Dock dual connector, but it's bigger and caps sharing at six units. Mixed-connector garage: Tesla. Tiny footprint or 10-unit sharing: Wallbox.

Versus the [Emporia Pro](/chargers/emporia-pro/): both do whole-home load management, but the Emporia bundles the panel monitor explicitly to *avoid* a panel upgrade — the more direct solution to a constrained panel — and costs less. The Wallbox counters with size and deeper multi-unit sharing.

## Bottom line

The Pulsar Plus is the charger you buy when the charger itself has to look good and think hard: smallest 48-amp footprint here, 10-unit power sharing nobody else offers, voice control, and Power Boost load management — for ~$650–$800 (check the current price; it moves). It's the priciest pick because the premium is design and intelligence, not power. One EV, no aesthetic constraints, tight budget? The EVIQO Gen 2 moves the same electrons for nearly half. Multi-EV household or a garage where the box is visible? This is the one.
